# Some Thoughts on Writing for the Internet

So we have been thinking a lot lately about the whole business of putting words on the internet, and it seems like there is an awful lot to say about it, honestly more than you might initially expect when you first sit down to consider the matter in any real depth.

The thing about content is that it is everywhere these days, and everybody seems to have an opinion about how you are supposed to do it, though when you actually get down to the specifics things become considerably more complicated and it turns out that a great many of those opinions tend to contradict one another in ways that are not always immediately obvious to the casual observer who has not spent a tremendous amount of time thinking carefully about the underlying dynamics at play here.

We reckon that the most important thing, if we had to pick just one thing out of everything, is probably that you should try to write in a way that feels natural and authentic and true to your own particular voice, because at the end of the day people can usually tell when something is not genuine, and that tends to undermine the whole endeavour in a fairly fundamental sort of way that is difficult to recover from once it has happened.

There are of course many other considerations that one might reasonably bring to bear on the question, and it would be remiss of us not to mention at least a few of them in passing, though we do not intend to dwell on any single one of them for terribly long given the constraints of the present discussion and the general desire to keep things moving along at a reasonable pace without getting too bogged down in the weeds.

Anyway, it is often said that the proof of the pudding is in the eating, and we suspect that the same broad principle applies rather neatly to the matter of writing things, in the sense that you really do not know how well a given piece is going to land until it has been out there in the world for a little while and people have had a proper chance to respond to it in their own time and in their own way.

In conclusion, or at least by way of drawing these rambling remarks towards something resembling a natural stopping point, we would simply reiterate that the whole thing is really quite a lot more involved than it might appear at first glance, and that anybody who tells you otherwise is probably trying to sell you something, which is a sentiment that we suspect most reasonable people would find themselves broadly in agreement with upon reflection.
